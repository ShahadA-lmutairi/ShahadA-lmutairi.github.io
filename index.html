Designed for Qiddiya

Qiddiya City is planned around one idea, the power of play, as a destination for entertainment, sports and culture. Each of my programs is built to fit that idea and the city's first cultural asset, the Qiddiya Performing Arts Centre.

Play at the coreEvery program has a playful step: guessing a proverb's meaning, adding your own voice, or choosing a favourite. Visitors take part instead of only watching.
A home in the Performing Arts CentreThe centre is planned to host more than 260 performances a year, with art galleries and educational entertainment. Proverb nights fit its stage, and the sound map and exhibition fit its galleries.
Immersive storytellingThe centre aims to lead in immersive storytelling with new technology. Voices of the Kingdom tells heritage stories through sound and touch.
Growing Saudi talentThe centre is meant to nurture young Saudi talent. Qiddiya Storytellers trains young Saudis as bilingual cultural guides.
Local and global visitorsArabic first, with English interpretation throughout, so Saudi families and international guests share the same experience.

The take-home: a sound card

Every visitor who records a proverb leaves with a sound card: the saying, its meaning, their region and a short clip. It is made for sharing, so the program keeps reaching people after they leave the city.

Sound cards also feed the reach measure: every share is counted.

Why this can work

  • UNESCO lists proverbs within oral traditions, one of the domains of intangible cultural heritage. Read UNESCO's page
  • Crowd-sourced sound maps are proven. The UK's "Sounds of our Shores" collected over 650 coastal recordings from the public in three months, all added to the British Library Sound Archive. See the project

The idea

Visitors stand before a large map of the Kingdom. They touch a region and hear how people there greet a guest, tell a proverb, or sing a work song. Every clip has an English caption that explains the meaning and the story behind it. Before leaving, visitors can record a proverb from their own hometown, and it joins a living archive that grows all season.

It draws on my background in applied linguistics: dialect is heritage, and hearing it is the fastest way to feel a place.

Objectives

  • Celebrate the Kingdom's spoken heritage in a way families enjoy
  • Open that heritage to international visitors through clear English interpretation
  • Turn visitors into contributors with a growing public archive

Who it is for

Saudi familiesWant a shared outing where kids and grandparents both have something to say. Hook: hearing their own region on the map.
International visitorsCurious but unsure where to start. Hook: short, captioned stories that make the culture feel open, not closed.
Young creatorsLove content they can share. Hook: recording a proverb and getting a shareable sound card.

Exhibition concept · 02

Said Our Way: Proverbs of Arabia

A 25-minute walk-through exhibition that shows how Saudi proverbs carry the values of the land and its people, from the desert and the sea to today's social media.

1 · OriginsWhere proverbs come from: oral tradition, poetry, and the majlis. Audio of elders reciting, with large calligraphy.
2 · The landProverbs about the desert, palms, rain and the sea. Natural materials, sand and palm frond textures, ambient sound.
3 · The peopleHospitality, generosity and patience. A small majlis corner where visitors hear proverbs in context.
4 · TodayHow proverbs live on in memes, songs and daily chat. A wall where visitors pick their favourite and see the live ranking.
DurationAbout 25 minutes
LanguagesArabic first, English interpretation throughout
AccessStep-free route, audio and large-text options
At QiddiyaSized for a gallery space such as those planned in the Performing Arts Centre
Link to program 01Visitors can continue to the Voices of the Kingdom sound map
